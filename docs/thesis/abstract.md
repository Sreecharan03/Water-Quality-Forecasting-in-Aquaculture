# Abstract (draft)

Fish farmers need early notice of falling dissolved oxygen, but forecasting pond water quality is hard to trust: models are often tested on data they have effectively seen, and simple guesses are rarely reported. This thesis builds and evaluates an explainable forecasting system for dissolved oxygen, pH and water temperature, 15 minutes to 6 hours ahead, with strict control against data leakage. Using 17 continuous pond sensor series from the Fish Welfare Initiative (Eluru, India; 15-minute readings, 28 November 2025 to 30 January 2026), we froze a time-and-pond split with a 12-hour embargo and four sealed ponds, and wrote every success rule before running each experiment.

Against two honest baselines ("same as now" and "same time yesterday"), a LightGBM model that learns a correction to a simple anchor beat both baselines in 5 of 9 target and distance combinations on validation ponds it had not seen. In the one-time sealed final test on four never-seen ponds over about ten later days, it clearly beat both baselines for oxygen 15 minutes and 1 hour ahead and temperature 1 hour ahead (3 of 5 model cells), but not at 6 hours, where "same time yesterday" was as good or better; those cells now use that simple guess. Data-quality information did not help consistently. Conformal prediction ranges never fell below 91.8% coverage in the final test but were usually wider than needed. A low-oxygen early-warning probability ranked risky moments better than three simple rules for the next 3 and 6 hours, yet its percentages were overstated and it raised false alarms on most days, so it is reported as experimental.

Exact TreeSHAP explanations were validated with pre-registered stability and faithfulness tests: 9 of 11 explanations passed, including all low-oxygen explanations. Repeating the pipeline on a second, very different real dataset (salt-marsh ponds in the USA) reproduced the method but not the oxygen gains: temperature improved, oxygen did not. A plain-language web application (FastAPI, HTML, CSS, JavaScript) refuses to forecast when readings are stuck, sparse or far outside the training range, and its numbers were verified to equal the research pipeline exactly.

The main contribution is an auditable evaluation protocol and an honest account of where machine learning helps pond forecasting and where simple guesses do as well. The results come from one region, one season and a small number of ponds, so they are evidence for the method, not a guarantee for other farms.

**Keywords:** aquaculture, dissolved oxygen, forecasting, explainable AI, SHAP, conformal prediction, leakage, pre-registration
